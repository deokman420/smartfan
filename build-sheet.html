Unplug the Pico before you wire anything

Every step below assumes the board is dead. Plug in only at the final check.

Three parts have a wrong way round

Electrolytic caps — the stripe is negative. The 1N4148 — the band goes to 5V. LEDs — long leg is the anode. Reversed caps vent; a reversed diode shorts the rail.

Your DHT22 is a 3-pin module, silkscreened VCC │ S │ GND

VCC nearest the Pico, then S, then GND — rows 27, 28, 29. The module carries its own pull-up, so there is no 10 kΩ to add. Double-check the printing before powering up: VCC and GND swapped will destroy the sensor.

Columns d–g are buried under the Pico on rows 1–20

The board is 21 mm wide but its pin rows are 17.78 mm apart, so it overhangs its own legs by about 1.6 mm each side. Legs sit in c and h; everything between them is covered. On any row the Pico occupies, land your wire in i or j — same node, but you can actually reach it.

Check your rails for a mid-board break

Look for a gap in the red and blue lines near row 30. If yours has one, bridge across it on all four rails before you start.

Before you plug in

Four meter checks that take a minute and catch the mistakes that cost parts. Probes on the board, Pico still unplugged, meter on continuity or 20k.

Continuity, Pico out of the board

  • Left + rail to left − rail: must NOT beep. A beep means the 100 µF is backwards or a wire has slipped a column.
  • Right + rail to right − rail: must NOT beep.
  • Left − to right −: must beep. That is your ground bridge, step 3.
  • Left + to right +: must NOT beep. These are 5 V and 3.3 V and they never touch.

Then plug in and measure

  • left + rail → GND reads about 5.0 V. VBUS is the raw USB rail — the Schottky drop belongs to VSYS (pin 39), not to VBUS. If this reads 0 V, step 4 is your fault.
  • right + rail → GND reads 3.3 V.
  • Anything above 3.4 V on the right rail: unplug immediately, you have 5 V where the DHT22 lives.
  • The fan should be still. A fan that spins the instant you plug in means the 100k pulldown is missing or the transistor legs are swapped.

Finding the S8050's legs with a meter

  • First, confirm it is actually an NPN. S8050 (NPN) and S8550 (PNP) are the complementary pair in identical TO-92 packages, and kits mix them. Out of circuit, diode mode, legs 1-2-3 with 2 in the middle: red on 2 reading ~0.7 to both outer legs means NPN — what this circuit needs. Black on 2 reading ~0.7 means PNP, and it will never work here in any orientation. Thirty seconds now saves hours later.
  • Which way does it face? The legs sit in a line down rows 36–38, so the flat can only point at the left rails or at the centre channel. Point it at the left rails. Stand where the flat is pointing and look back at it: your right hand now points down the board toward higher row numbers, so "E, B, C left to right" lands as E 36, B 37, C 38.
  • The printed marking is tiny and the package is symmetrical, so orientation by eye is still guesswork. The meter settles it in about twenty seconds.
  • Find the base. Diode-test mode. Put the red probe on one leg and touch black to each of the others. When one leg reads about 0.7 to both others, that leg is the base — and it confirms the part is NPN. On this package the base is the middle leg.
  • Then split collector from emitter. Red still on the base: the emitter reads a few tens of millivolts higher than the collector, typically 0.75 vs 0.70. Small, but consistent.
  • Or skip the subtlety. If your meter has an hFE socket, drop it into the NPN side. A reading of 160–300 means you guessed right; under 10 means the outer two legs are swapped.
  • Or just try it. Backwards, the transistor has a gain near 3 instead of 200, so the fan sits still or barely stirs. Nothing is damaged at 5 V through a 1 kΩ base resistor — swap the outer legs and go again.

If something is wrong

  • No conduction at all, collector stranded high. Measure the base at row 37 with the fan commanded on. Around 0.03–0.1 V means the base resistor and the pulldown are swapped: a 100 kΩ feeding the base against a 1 kΩ pulldown forms a 100:1 divider that never reaches the 0.7 V turn-on threshold. The base resistor (34 b→37 b) must be the 1 kΩ. Measure both with one leg lifted — in circuit you are reading the junction and the other resistor in parallel.
  • Fan spins but rpm reads 0. The giveaway for a transistor that is conducting but not saturating. The fan gets ~3 V instead of 4.8 V so it turns, but the collector sits near 2 V — and the tach is open-collector referenced to that node, so its "low" is 2 V and the Pico reads it as high. Cause is nearly always base current: check the 1 kΩ is the base resistor (34 b→37 b) and the 100 kΩ is the pulldown (37 a→rail). Swapped, you get 25 µA of drive instead of 2.6 mA. Their colour bands differ in one place only — the fourth, brown for 1 k, orange for 100 k.
  • Fan completely still. Swap the two outer transistor legs. A reversed NPN has a gain near 3 instead of 200 — not destructive, so it is a safe thing to simply try.
  • Halve the problem instead of guessing. Pull the fan's black lead out of 38 e and put it straight into the left − rail. That bypasses the transistor entirely. Spins → rail, fan and leads are good, so the fault is the switch or its drive. Still dead → VBUS is not reaching the left + rail (step 4), or the red lead, or the fan.
  • DHT22 always fails. Almost always the module's pin order. Unplug, check the silkscreen against your wiring, and confirm the module's power LED lights when the rail is live.
  • Pico resets when the fan starts. The salvaged 100 µF has lost its capacitance. Replace it.
  • Everything reads 0 rpm even when it is clearly spinning. Make sure the board is running the current calibrate.py. An earlier version counted pulses with list.__setitem__ in the interrupt handler — which CPython allows and MicroPython does not, so it threw once per pulse and reported a clean-looking zero.

Commissioning

What is already on the board, what this build actually measured, and the one thing still left to calibrate.

Firmware on the Pico

  • MicroPython v1.29.0, built 2026-08-24 for RPI_PICO2_W, ARM — not the RISC-V image.
  • Enumerates as 2E8A:0005. Before this it ran gusmanb LogicAnalyzer v6.0, which registered its own USB id and no REPL; that image is backed up under _firmware-backups/.
  • On the filesystem: config.py, main.py, calibrate.py. main.py autoruns at power-up.
  • dht, network and bluetooth are all present, so phase 2 reporting needs no reflash.

Measured on this build

  • Fan 3,840 rpm — 128 tach pulses/s, steady to within one pulse. Rated 4,500 at a true 5 V; USB rails usually sit nearer 4.8 V, which accounts for the difference.
  • DHT22 52.7 %RH, 24.2 °C, dew point 13.9 °C. Baseline locked after the 60-second LEARN pass.
  • LDR ~22,000 raw at room ambient.
  • Fan pin reads 0 at boot — the 100 kΩ pulldown is doing its job.

Still to calibrate

  • The light thresholds ship at LIGHT_ON 30000 / LIGHT_OFF 20000, and ambient here measured ~22,000 — that sits in the dead band between them. These numbers are placeholders and must be set in the real bathroom.
  • Run calibrate.light() in the actual mounting position, fixture on for half the window and off for the other half, then put its suggested values into config.py.
  • Until that is done the fan will not respond to the light at all, and only humidity will start it.

Bench commands

  • calibrate.leds() — blinks both LEDs, echoes the button.
  • calibrate.fan() — drives the fan, reports rpm each second.
  • calibrate.sensor() — ten DHT22 reads; occasional checksum errors are normal.
  • calibrate.light() — LDR sweep with suggested thresholds.
  • Run one with mpremote connect COM5 exec "import calibrate; calibrate.fan()". Ctrl-C gets you a REPL even while main.py is looping.

What the LEDs tell you

Two LEDs carry the states, so you can read the controller from the doorway without a serial cable. The slow green flash is not a sixth state: the controller is in IDLE, and the odor pixel has not settled yet.

LEARN — first 60 s, building the humidity baseline. Fast, even blink
IDLE, settling — one short green flash every 2.4 s. The odor pixel has not gone flat yet
IDLE — fan off, odor pixel settled, or no odor sensor
RUN — fan on, light and/or humidity
BOOST — 15 min manual override from the button
FAULT — DHT22 silent, or fan commanded on but not turning
