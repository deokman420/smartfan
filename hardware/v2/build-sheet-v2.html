V2 Build Sheet

120 V AC stops at a module inside the metal box. A Mean Well IRM-10-12 makes 12 V for a Noctua NF-F12, a Recom buck makes 5 V for the Pico, and the S8050 off the V1 board stops switching the fan’s ground and starts driving a high-side MOSFET instead. The button, both LEDs, the LDR and the SHT40 move onto the grille, on two cables. Humidity moves from the DHT22 to an SHT40 on I²C. Pick a step to isolate its wire; pick a part for its readings; switch the operating state to see what a meter should say at that moment.

Nothing here is bought yet

This is the wiring half of hardware/v2/prototype-v2.md. Parts and prices are in hardware/v2/shopping-list-v2.html. The V1 breadboard stays as it is — it is the reference this build has to beat, not a donor to strip. The one part that changes role is the S8050, and so far only on paper.

The board is drawn. hardware/v2 is a two‑layer PCB that carries every connection on this sheet as copper, so the steps below are the circuit to check, not wires to run. The wires you still make are on hardware/v2/wiring-v2.html. On 2026‑11‑08 the board changed four things this sheet now follows: mains lands in a screw terminal, J5; the fan plugs into a keyed header, J2; the LED resistors sit on the anode side; and the button has a 1 kΩ series resistor, R9. Where this sheet and hardware/v2/README.md disagree, the README wins.

Mains is in this box, and there is no earth pin to land

The cord is a figure‑8, NEMA 1‑15P to IEC C7, into a C8 inlet. Unplug it before touching the AC end, and treat the fuse holder as live until the fuse is out. The fuse sits in the hot lead only. The supply is Class II: there is no protective earth, and running a wire from a mains contact to the aluminum box does not create one — it gives a fault a path into the case.

This fan’s colors are not the V1 colors

On the NF‑A4x10, yellow is tach and red is 5 V. On this NF‑F12, yellow is switched +12 V, green is tach, blue is a 5 V PWM input, and there is no red lead. Copying the V1 sheet onto this fan puts 12 V on GP18, which is a destroyed pin.

Two diodes, two bands, two different failures

The 1N5819 band points at the Pico, into VSYS pin 39 — pin 40 is VBUS, the corner by the USB socket, and the buck never lands there. The 1N4148 band sits on the yellow wire; reversed, it shorts 12 V to 0 V the moment the fan turns on. And the IRF9540’s metal tab is the drain, not a heatsink you can screw down: insulate it from the box.

The humidity sensor is the one piece of new firmware

No DHT22 is soldered into this build. An SHT40 on I²C takes its place, which needs sht4x.py and the one function in main.py that reads climate. Ship that swap on the V1 breadboard first — it is one function with an unchanged contract, verifiable against the sensor-fault tests that already exist — then build V2 against an image that is already proven. Let the baseline re-learn for fifteen minutes afterwards: an SHT40 reads 1–3 % away from where the DHT22 sat.

Three config entries become lies, and one pin is freed

GP17 high still means fan on, TACH_PPR = 2 still matches a Noctua, and MIN_RPM = 800 still sits under this fan’s 2000 rpm — the fan path is untouched. But PIN_TACH says the tach wire is yellow, and here it is green. STUCK_S = 12 was timed on a 40 mm rotor, so time this one’s coast before trusting it. And PIN_DHT = 22 is deleted outright: humidity moves to PIN_SDA = 0 and PIN_SCL = 1. Keep SAMPLE_S = 2 — it is no longer forced by the sensor, but BASELINE_A, STUCK_S, SPINUP_S and the tach window are all reasoned against that cadence.

What the meter says, by state

Follows the selector in the schematic. Black probe on 0 V throughout.

Continuity, cord out, before first power

  • +12 to 0 V does not beep. Nor does buck pin 3 to 0 V, nor pin 36 to pin 38.
  • Yellow to any GPIO does not beep. Yellow’s only partners are the MOSFET drain and the banded end of the 1N4148.
  • Gate to source reads 100 kΩ — that is the pull-up, and it is the one resistor whose absence lets the fan start on its own. Gate to 0 V must not be a short.
  • Pin 40 has no soldered wire on it at all.
  • SDA to SCL does not beep, and neither of them reaches 0 V. Both idle high through the breakout’s own pull-ups.
  • 0 V to the box is open. So is the MOSFET tab to the box.

S8050, before it is soldered

  • Diode mode, red probe on the middle leg: about 0.7 to both outer legs means NPN, which is what this circuit needs.
  • Black on the middle reading 0.7 means it is an S8550. That part turns on when the base goes low and does not belong here in any orientation.
  • With red still on the base, the emitter is the outer leg reading a few tens of millivolts higher. Emitter to 0 V, collector to the 100 Ω.
  • Backwards it has a gain near 3 instead of 200: the gate barely moves and the fan sits still. Nothing is damaged — swap the outer legs and go again.

Not on this build

  • PWM speed control. Blue is tied to 5 V so the fan is full on whenever GP17 is on. A low blue pin stops this fan, so the tie is required, not optional.
  • The SGP41 odor path. Its bus is wired and its address is free; the sensor is a later purchase. Costed in docs/sgp41-odor-sensing.md.
  • The DHT22. It stays on the V1 breadboard and is never soldered into this build.
  • The 100 µF the V1 fan needed across USB. Holding the 12 V rail up is the IRM‑10’s job. Recom shows an optional input capacitor on the buck; it is not on the shopping list.
  • A printed tray, and the NF‑A4x10. The small fan stays on the V1 breadboard. The washable filter and the grille-mounted sensors are in the housing brief, not on this sheet — see hardware/v2/model-v2.html and chassis-and-housing.md.

Will it actually move air

A 120 mm case fan has plenty of airflow and almost no pressure, and a duct is pressure. Drag the restriction to see what each fan has left. Free‑air and maximum‑pressure figures are Noctua’s published numbers; the points between them are read off their curve, which is where HVI rates bath fans.

0.10 in w.g.

12 V budget

Noctua for the iPPC‑2000 IP67: 72 CFM free air, 3.94 mm H₂O maximum, 29.7 dBA, 0.1 A. The iPPC‑3000 fits the same 120 × 25 mm pocket and the same 105 mm screw pitch: 110 CFM, 7.63 mm H₂O, 43.5 dBA. Swapping to it changes nothing in the electronics — still 12 V, still inside the module’s 0.85 A. The Panasonic FV‑08VQ5 is a centrifugal bath fan, drawn only for scale. 25.4 mm H₂O = 1 in w.g.

12 V ±2.5 %, 0.85 A, Class II and the 3 kV isolation are from the Mean Well IRM‑10 spec, which points at the installation manual for the fuse value and the clearances rather than printing them — so the 2 A time‑lag is the shopping‑list choice, sized over the 0.25 A run current at 115 V and under the 20 A cold inrush, and the clearance around the module is whatever that manual says. R‑78E pin order is from the Recom datasheet. IRF9540 lead order — gate, drain, source, with the tab on the drain — is from the Infineon TO‑220 drawing. Fan figures are Noctua’s published specification. Pico pins match config.py. Enclosure footprints are datasheet outlines drawn to scale, and nothing here has been measured against a part in hand.
