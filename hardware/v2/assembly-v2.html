Decide the air path before you print

The model offers two, and it offers them as a toggle — the turning elbow is shroud.visible = false in the viewer, with its own set of streamlines. They are alternatives drawn in the same space, never both at once, which is why nothing complains on screen.

As parts they foul each other. The collar's inboard stub and the elbow's tail are both Ø104.8 and both want the 22 mm of bore between z 53 and the wall at 75. Pick one before printing, because the choice changes which files you print:

What must not change

1.6 mm over the elbow's crown. The bend tops out at y 149.4 and the box's inside is 151. Thicken that wall and the part stops fitting the box it was dimensioned for.

The bore stays 100. Every wall on this build goes outward for the same reason: the fan has 0.155 in w.g. in total and the easy-duct case already spends 0.10 of it for about 20 CFM. There is no bore area to give back.

The duct leaves along the bay, not across it. A 100 mm hole through a 2×6 is not permitted — a third of 140 is about 46 — and would take the joist with it. The fan's orientation follows from that, not from preference.

If the filter stays, the fan should be the iPPC-3000. A clean pad costs 0.02–0.05 in w.g. and a loaded one more, which on the 2000 is most or all of the remaining margin. Same 120 × 25 pocket, same 105 screws; 43.5 dBA against 29.7 is the trade.

Do not bolt the elbow's lip to the fan. The four holes on its 105 square are left from an earlier idea about sharing the fan's fixings, and that idea is wrong: it would take the elbow out with the cartridge, and the cartridge dropping out on its own is the thing the whole layout is for. The elbow is held by its wall flange. The lip holes carry nothing.

The grille cables enter the 1590BB on the end walls. The face toward the fan is crossed by a hanger bar, and a gland nut will not sit on that wall. On the grille, the button hole is the cable passage. The sensor cable uses the fitting on the opposite end of the box.
