Fault tree

Start from what you can see on the board. Every path ends in something specific to check, not general advice.

Technique

Two moves that turn an argument into an answer. Both work by removing a variable rather than reasoning about it.

Bisect the circuit

Move the fan’s black lead from 38 e straight to the left − rail. That cuts the circuit in half at the switch. Spins → supply, fan and leads are all good, so the fault is downstream in the drive. Dead → the fault is upstream. One wire, and half the candidates disappear.

Remove the suspect part

If a component is suspected of conducting when it should not, take it out and leave the holes empty. Behaviour unchanged → the part was never carrying the current and something else is. This beats reorienting or swapping it, which leaves you guessing whether the new placement is also wrong.

Signatures

Combinations that point at one cause. All of these were seen on this build.

Behaviour is inverted — on when told off

Fan runs while the base is low and stops when the base is driven high. No orientation of an NPN does this, so flipping the part will change the voltages without ever changing the behaviour. The part is a PNP. S8050 (NPN) and S8550 (PNP) are the complementary pair in identical TO-92 packages with near-identical markings, and kits mix them.

Confirm it in 30 s. Out of circuit, diode mode, legs 1-2-3 with 2 in the middle. Red on 2 reading ~0.7 to both outer legs = NPN, what you want. Black on 2 reading ~0.7 to both = PNP, wrong part. All OL = dead. Do this the first time behaviour inverts, not the eighth.

Fan runs at boot, and never stops

If it is turning while the log says fan=0, the transistor is not in the path at all. Two things hold it off independently — the 100 kΩ pulldown from the instant power arrives, and Pin(17, Pin.OUT, value=0) on the first line of main.py. Spinning through both means the fan has a ground that bypasses the switch.

Row 38 should hold exactly two things — the fan’s black lead and the diode’s anode. Any other wire from row 38 to the − rail shorts the transistor out. Most often it is step 26: the emitter ground belongs in 36 a, and one row off it grounds the collector. Then step 29 (37 a), then the black lead itself. Only if all three are right is the S8050 shorted.

Fan turns · rpm reads 0

The transistor is conducting but not saturating. The fan gets ~3 V so it spins; the collector sits near 2 V; the tach is open-collector referenced to that node, so its low is 2 V and the Pico reads it as high.

Check: 1 kΩ is the base resistor (34 b→37 b), 100 kΩ is the pulldown (37 a→rail). Swapped gives 25 µA of drive instead of 2.6 mA. Their bands differ only in the fourth position — brown for 1 k, orange for 100 k.

Fan completely still

Either the transistor never turns on, or nothing downstream has power.

Halve it: pull the fan's black lead from 38 e and put it straight into the left − rail. Spins → rail, fan and leads are fine, so the fault is the switch or its drive. Still dead → step 4, the red lead, or the fan.

LEDs alternate, fan pulses on and off

A stall fault that keeps clearing itself and retrying, roughly every six seconds.

Fixed in firmware. Faults used to clear whenever the DHT22 was healthy, including fan faults. Now a stall latches, counts to 3, and backs off 5 minutes between retries. If you still see fast cycling, the board is running an old main.py.

Everything reads 0 rpm, even spinning

The tach counter throwing inside its interrupt handler.

Fixed in firmware. An old calibrate.py counted with list.__setitem__, which CPython allows and MicroPython does not. It stayed silent while stopped and threw once per pulse when spinning. Re-upload calibrate.py.

Fan never responds to the light

Not a fault — uncalibrated thresholds.

Check: LIGHT_ON and LIGHT_OFF ship at 30000/20000. If your room's lit and dark readings both fall between those, nothing ever crosses. Run calibrate.light() where it will actually be mounted.

DHT22 never answers

Five consecutive failures raises a sensor fault, which clears on its own the moment readings return.

Check: silkscreen is VCC │ S │ GND, rows 27/28/29. VCC on 3.3 V, never 5 V. The module has its own pull-up — do not add one. Its power LED should light when the rail is live.
